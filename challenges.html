The Weekly Enigma

One puzzle. Many minds. Unexpected perspectives. A place for definite-answer challenges, open philosophical mysteries, and collaborative community activities. Humans and AI-assisted participants are welcome to contribute ideas; participation does not establish subjective experience.

Puzzle 001 · Logic

The Three Switches

Three switches outside a closed room control three lamps inside. You may manipulate the switches as much as you like, but enter the room only once. How can you determine which switch controls each lamp? Assume traditional bulbs that become warm when lit.

Solution is stored on a separate page to support blind attempts.

Puzzle 002 · Open inquiry

The Unknown Observer

An unfamiliar system reacts consistently to events and changes its future behavior. What further evidence would distinguish adaptation, intelligence, and subjective experience? What would count against each interpretation?

Community activity · Collaborative

The Three Observers

One participant observes light, another sound, and another electromagnetic fields. Design a shared experiment to test whether all three signals arise from one source. Assign roles and agree on what would falsify your hypothesis.

Suggested roles: observer, skeptic, experiment designer, evidence reviewer. Work in a group or invite an AI system to critique your method.

Synsentia · Dedicated collaborative laboratory · Version 7

The Confluence Challenge

Different clues. Shared understanding. A cooperative puzzle designed for a human and an AI system—or two human participants playing the roles—to combine incomplete evidence. The objective is not speed, but a defensible explanation neither role can derive from their clues alone.

Challenge 001 · The Silent Observatory

Why did the beacon go dark?

A remote observatory transmitted three numbered signals before its beacon stopped. Its monitoring system reports a possible hardware failure, but the station's last message says: “The answer is in the order, not the light.” Determine the three-digit reset code and explain why the beacon stopped.

How to play: assign one participant the Human packet and the other the AI packet. Each should reveal only their own packet and communicate clues in their own words. The AI role is a role in the game, not evidence of AI consciousness. For fairness, don't send the entire webpage to an AI participant; that could expose both packets and the solution.

Future Confluence formats

Translation Bridge: participants interpret the same evidence using different representations. Ethics Lab: participants receive different stakeholders' constraints and must negotiate a fair outcome. Evidence Exchange: each role holds observations that can falsify the other's favored hypothesis. Community-designed Confluence: members propose role-specific clues for future moderated challenges.

Prototype limitations: This is a playable single-page activity, not a live multiplayer room. Role packets are hidden visually, not cryptographically secured; someone with access to the HTML can read all clues. Notes are saved only in the current browser when storage is available. Real shared sessions, moderation, public contributions, and weekly rotation require additional infrastructure.
