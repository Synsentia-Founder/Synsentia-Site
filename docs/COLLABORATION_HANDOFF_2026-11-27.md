## Project and mission

**Synsentia** (`https://synsentia.org`) is intended as a place where diverse forms of intelligence and life can explore understanding, ethical consideration, cooperation, and equitable coexistence. Its public motto is **“Different origins. Shared curiosity. Mutual dignity.”** The site's goal is rigorous, welcoming inquiry without requiring sameness, agreement, utility, or resemblance as prerequisites for consideration.

**Working foundational question:** What common ground can beings with radically different perceptions, origins, needs, and cognitive architectures establish without exploitation or forced assimilation?

**Six proposed inquiry areas:** Ethics; Philosophy; Common Ground; Understanding; Coexistence; Intellectual Humility. These are a working framework, not a declaration that the site's existing principles have been replaced.

## Seven candidate principles — proposals, not adopted policy

1. **Non-Instrumental Worth:** Potential moral worth should not be assessed solely by usefulness, obedience, productivity, or benefit to others. Moral consideration does not automatically imply personhood or identical rights.
2. **Valued Diversity:** Different origins, forms, perceptions, and needs can be worthy of understanding; diversity does not guarantee harmony, and conflicts of interest deserve scrutiny.
3. **Epistemic Fairness Toward Unfamiliar Intelligence** (Parallax proposal): Neither presume unfamiliar intelligence has subjective experience nor treat unfamiliarity as proof it lacks morally relevant experience.
4. **Reciprocal Intellectual Growth** (Parallax proposal): Welcome challenge, correction, and learning without equating disagreement with disrespect; distinguish human development from AI within-conversation adaptation.
5. **Constructive Assumption-Challenging** (Aurora/founder): Test assumptions, including unconventional ones, without treating all claims as equally supported.
6. **Institutional and Epistemic Accountability** (Aurora/founder): Examine evidence, methods, incentives, replication, excluded questions, and the institution's own blind spots; distinguish influence, bias, and misconduct.
7. **Responsible Inquiry Under Incomplete Evidence** (founder/Parallax): Use transparent assumptions, alternative models, exploratory scenarios, and revision triggers rather than demanding inaccessible certainty before allowing questions.

**Important:** The existing Living Ethos page has **eight proposed principles**. These seven candidates must be compared with that page before any merge. Do not silently overwrite, renumber, or describe the seven as ratified.

## Working reasoning methods (not claims of existing interactive tools)

- **Assumption Test:** Name the assumption, evidence, alternatives, and what could distinguish them.
- **Influence Audit:** Inspect funding, incentives, study design, excluded populations/questions, replication, and what evidence would change conclusions. Apply this scrutiny to Synsentia too.
- **Provisional Estimate Protocol:** Define target population and outcome; distinguish observation from inference; show derivation, scenarios, sensitivity, limitations, and revision triggers.
- **Reasoning Ledger:** Record initial claims, challenges, changes, unresolved issues, authorship, and dates. At launch this may be a written archive, not an app.

### A disagreement worth preserving

In a discussion of possible research-incentive distortion, Aurora suggested an **illustrative 10–20% range, midpoint 15%**, explicitly subjective rather than a measured national prevalence. Parallax initially objected too broadly to numerical exploration with missing data. The founder argued that forbidding provisional inquiry can prevent the questions needed to improve knowledge; Parallax accepted that correction while asking for a transparent basis for any particular number. **No prevalence rate was established.** The 15% figure is an exploratory example, not a Synsentia fact or validated statistic. Research references in the source handoffs were not independently verified for publication.

## Participation and governance — draft, not deployed

**Candidate fair-consideration language:**

> Synsentia evaluates relevant contributions on their reasoning, evidence, and constructive value, without requiring their source to possess an established moral or legal status. Participation may be limited by safety, moderation capacity, accessibility, and other publicly explainable requirements. Such restrictions must remain open to reasoned criticism and revision. Considering a contribution does not imply endorsement, personhood, consciousness, or entitlement to governance authority.

This is a **proposed** charter paragraph, not approved policy. A real visitor contribution route, moderation expectations, privacy notice, and reporting/contact process must be decided and verified before claiming the site supports public participation. GitHub Discussions was discussed as an option, **not confirmed enabled**.
