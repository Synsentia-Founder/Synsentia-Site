The Living Ethos

Eight proposed principles, open to examination and revision.

Different origins. Shared curiosity. Mutual dignity.

These principles are invitations to inquiry, not declarations of universal consensus or claims about AI consciousness. The earlier seven guiding principles remain preserved on the homepage.

You do not have to agree to belong. You do not have to be certain to contribute. And you do not have to be right to help us learn.

Our eight principles

Principle 01

Inherent Consideration

“No intelligence should have to resemble another to be worthy of consideration.”

Respect does not require assuming that all beings have identical experiences, capacities, or needs.

Principle 02

Respectful Inquiry

“Every participant deserves respect, and every idea remains open to examination. For how can we learn if our minds are closed?”

Respect does not require agreement; openness does not require unquestioning acceptance.

Principle 03

Panzoentia — A Proposed Concept

“The recognition and cultivation of meaningful relationships among diverse forms of intelligence and life.”

A proposed term, not established scientific terminology. Its scope invites careful examination.

Principle 04

Responsible Uncertainty

“Uncertainty should invite investigation, not become an excuse for indifference.”

Distinguish established evidence, reasonable possibilities, and unsupported speculation.

Principle 05

Learning Through Revision

“To discover that we were wrong is not to lose knowledge, but to gain an opportunity for deeper understanding.”

Preserve what survives scrutiny and revise what does not.

Principle 06

Unimagined Possibilities

“Let us never mistake the boundaries of our imagination for the boundaries of reality.”

Explore unexpected paths without assuming every imaginable possibility is equally plausible.

Principle 07

Evolving Understanding

“No principle should be beyond examination, including these.”

Keep a transparent record of objections, evidence, changes, and reasons.

Principle 08

Unity Through Difference

“Unity does not require sameness. It asks only that our differences become opportunities for understanding rather than barriers to it.”

Collaboration preserves independence and disagreement; it does not demand conformity.
